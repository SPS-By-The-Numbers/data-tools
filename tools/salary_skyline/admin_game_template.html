Fine print

  • Data: OSPI S-275 personnel report, Seattle Public Schools, 2024–25 final (sps-btn-data.safs_s275) — the same pull as the salary skyline. One bubble per employee; bubble area, bar heights, and medians are total compensation — total_final_salary plus the F-196-derived benefits load (see below).
  • “SEA-represented” is approximated from OSPI duty codes (teachers 31–34, substitutes 52, certificated support 39–49 & 63–64, paraeducators 91, office & clerical 94). Real bargaining-unit rosters differ at the margins. Custodial, trades and food service staff belong to other unions and are left out of both sides of the game.
  • Benefits are always included, from F-196 actuals. SPS’s 2024–25 F-196 reports @@OBJ4M@@ of Object 4 (Employee Benefits & Payroll Taxes) against @@OBJ23M@@ of salary Objects 2+3. S-275 final salaries total @@S275M@@, so scaling Object 4 by that ratio attributes @@BENPOOLM@@ of benefits to S-275-covered pay — @@BEN_PCT@@¢ per salary dollar. Every staff figure in the game — bubble areas, skyline bars, medians, and the money a cut frees — is salary × @@BENMULT3@@. Purchased services carry no benefits load. Source: sps-btn-data.safs_f19x.general_fund_expenditures, actuals, objects 2/3/4.
  • The dashboard skyline draws one bar per person at a single shared width and dollar scale across all sections, so section widths compare headcounts directly (which is why the central-office block is a thin strip next to 5,743 SEA staff) and bar heights compare pay. If you hand a small group an enormous pool, its blue stack can run off the top of the chart — the panel numbers stay exact.
  • With “Show program splits” on, the SEA side of the skyline is split by program, assigning each employee to the bucket holding the plurality of their assignment FTE (ties go to basic; zero-FTE staff use assignment-row plurality): LAP is OSPI program 55, Title I is programs 51–53, and Special education is programs 21+24 (combined because duty-46 school psychologists moved from program 24 to 21 in 2022–23); everything else is basic ed + other. Among SEA-represented staff that gives @@PGROUP_COUNTS@@. Redistribution weights stay per staff group — the program split is a lens, not a control.
  • The hole comes from the district’s own 2026–27 budget book (26-27-Budget-Book-Online.pdf). The $@@DEFICIT@@ budget deficit is the General Fund gap between 2026–27 expenditures and revenues + other financing (PDF p.14, confirmed as the “calculated gap” on p.20). The Economic Stabilization Fund shows $0 committed in every 2026–27 fund-balance column (PDF p.21); board policy floors it at 3% of the total budget, and 3% of the $@@GF_BUDGET@@ General Fund expenditure budget is $@@ESF_TARGET@@. One honesty note: the deficit gap and the stabilization refill are one-time 2026–27 amounts, while a cut position frees money every year — this game shows a single year.
  • Purchased services come from the same F-196 actuals: General Fund Object 7, split into a special-ed partition (programs 21+24) and everything else, one bubble per NCES spending class sized by the summed annual amount (tools/salary_skyline/services_query.sql). The same dollars-per-area scale as the salary bubbles, so contracted student transportation ($59.7M) dwarfs any administrator. The benefits toggle does not apply to services, and they are excluded from the one-bar-per-person skyline. Cutting a service class zeroes a whole year of that spending — judge for yourself which contracts a district could actually live without.
  • Small bubbles and short bars are usually partial-year hires, not low-paid roles.
  • This is a thought experiment: crossing out a position moves its money and ignores its work. Nothing here is a budget proposal.
